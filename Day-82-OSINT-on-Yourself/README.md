Day 82 – OSINT on Yourself
🎯 Objective

To understand Open Source Intelligence (OSINT), identify what personal information may be publicly available, understand how attackers can use that information, and learn how to reduce personal digital exposure.

📚 Lesson of the Day

OSINT stands for Open Source Intelligence.

It means collecting and analyzing information from publicly available sources.

OSINT does not mean open-source software.

It also does not require hacking or unauthorized access.

Anyone can potentially collect publicly available information from sources such as:

Search engines
Social media
Public websites
Blogs
Public records
News articles
Publicly available databases
Metadata
🔍 What Is OSINT?

OSINT is the process of gathering useful information from sources that are openly available.

Public Information
       ↓
   Collection
       ↓
    Analysis
       ↓
Intelligence

For cybersecurity, OSINT can be used by both:

Defenders
Security researchers
Investigators
Attackers

The same information that helps defenders understand exposure can also help attackers perform reconnaissance.

🌐 Common OSINT Sources
1. Search Engines

Search engines can reveal publicly indexed information about:

People
Organizations
Websites
Documents
News
Public profiles
2. Social Media

Public social media profiles may reveal:

Names
Job roles
Education
Interests
Locations
Photos
Professional relationships

Even small pieces of information can become useful when combined with other information.

3. Public Websites and Blogs

Websites and blogs may contain:

Author names
Contact information
Company information
Technical information
Announcements
Project details
4. Public Records

Depending on the country and applicable laws, some records may be publicly accessible.

These can provide additional information about individuals or organizations.

5. News Articles

News reports can reveal information about:

Organizations
Employees
Events
Business relationships
Technology
Security incidents
🎯 Why Is OSINT Important?

One of the biggest reasons to perform OSINT on yourself is to understand your real digital exposure.

You may know what you intentionally share online, but you may not realize how much information can be discovered by combining different public sources.

For example:

Social Media
     +
Search Engines
     +
Public Websites
     +
Public Records
     ↓
Combined Information
     ↓
Digital Profile

Each individual piece of information may appear harmless.

Together, they can create a much more detailed picture.

🕵️ How Attackers Use OSINT

Attackers may collect publicly available information before attempting an attack.

This activity is called reconnaissance.

It is one of the earliest stages of many attacks.

Public Information
       ↓
   OSINT Collection
       ↓
   Target Research
       ↓
Reconnaissance
       ↓
Potential Attack
1️⃣ Targeted Phishing

OSINT can help attackers create more personalized phishing messages.

Instead of sending a generic message, an attacker may use publicly available information to make a message appear more believable.

For example, publicly available information about:

Job role
Company
Colleagues
Projects

could potentially be used to make a phishing message more convincing.

Defense

Be cautious even when a message contains information that appears to be correct.

Personalized does not automatically mean legitimate.

2️⃣ Password Reset and Security Questions

Public information may sometimes reveal details that people use in security questions.

Examples include:

Pet's name
Birthday
Hometown
School name
Favorite team

If an account relies on easily discoverable information for authentication or recovery, attackers may try to use that information.

Defense
Use strong passwords
Use a password manager
Enable MFA
Avoid predictable security answers
Use account recovery methods that do not rely on easily discoverable information
3️⃣ Social Engineering

Attackers can use publicly available information to make social engineering attempts more convincing.

For example, knowing someone's:

Job role
Organization
Professional contacts
Interests
Recent activities

may help an attacker create a believable story.

The goal is often to make the target trust the attacker.

4️⃣ Planning Larger Attacks

OSINT may also be used as preparation for larger attacks.

Attackers can use public information to understand:

Organization structure
Employees
Technologies
Relationships
Public-facing systems
Potential targets

This information can support later attack stages.

🔎 What's Findable About You?

Attackers can combine information from multiple public sources.

Common sources include:

Social Media

Public posts, profiles, photos, connections, and professional information.

Data Breaches

Previously exposed information may become available through leaked or compromised databases.

Search Engines

Search engines can reveal publicly indexed pages, documents, profiles, and other information.

Public Records

Depending on applicable laws and availability, public records can reveal additional information.

Metadata

Files and photos can sometimes contain metadata that provides additional information.

🧩 Information Aggregation

The biggest OSINT lesson is that small pieces of information can become more significant when combined.

For example:

Name
 +
Company
 +
Job Role
 +
Public Email
 +
Social Media
 +
Location Information
      ↓
Detailed Digital Profile

An attacker does not necessarily need one large piece of information.

They may build a profile by combining many small pieces.

🛡️ Turning OSINT Into Defense

The purpose of performing OSINT on yourself is not to become scared of the internet.

The goal is to understand your exposure and reduce unnecessary risk.

A simple approach is:

Know Your Exposure
        ↓
Reduce Your Exposure
        ↓
Stay Aware
1️⃣ Know Your Exposure

Search for information about yourself using legitimate public sources.

Look for:

Your name
Public profiles
Old accounts
Public email addresses
Photos
Public documents
Information about your workplace or education

Ask:

What could a stranger learn about me from public sources?

2️⃣ Reduce Your Exposure

Once you understand what is publicly available, reduce unnecessary exposure.

Possible actions include:

Make unnecessary social media information private
Remove outdated public information where practical
Delete unused accounts
Avoid sharing sensitive personal information
Review old posts
Avoid exposing unnecessary contact information
Remove sensitive metadata when appropriate

The goal is not to remove yourself from the internet completely.

The goal is to control unnecessary exposure.

3️⃣ Stay Aware

Digital exposure changes over time.

New information may become public through:

New social media posts
New jobs
Public documents
Data breaches
New websites
Professional profiles

Therefore, OSINT awareness should be an ongoing habit.

Review
  ↓

  ↓

  ↓

  ↓
Repeat
