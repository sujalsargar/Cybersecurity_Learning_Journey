Lesson of the Day

Today, I learned what a Security Operations Center (SOC) is and what a SOC Analyst does during a typical workday.

A SOC is the frontline defense of an organization, where security professionals continuously monitor systems, investigate alerts, and respond to cyber threats.

A simple way to remember today's lesson is:

A SOC watches over an organization's digital environment 24/7, and SOC analysts investigate anything that looks suspicious.

What is a SOC?

A Security Operations Center (SOC) is a dedicated team responsible for monitoring and protecting an organization's cybersecurity.

Many SOCs operate 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, ensuring that security incidents are detected and handled as quickly as possible.

The SOC works with security tools such as:

SIEM platforms
IDS/IPS
Endpoint security solutions
Firewalls
Threat intelligence feeds

Their goal is to detect, investigate, and respond to cyber threats before they cause significant damage.

Who is a SOC Analyst?

A SOC Analyst is a cybersecurity professional responsible for monitoring security alerts, investigating suspicious activity, and helping protect an organization's systems.

SOC analysts are often the first people to notice signs of an attack.

They analyze alerts, determine whether they represent real threats, and take appropriate action.

SOC Analyst Levels

Today's lesson introduced the three common SOC analyst tiers.

Tier 1 - Monitoring and Initial Investigation

Tier 1 is usually the entry-level SOC role.

Responsibilities include:

Monitoring security alerts
Reviewing SIEM dashboards
Performing initial investigations
Identifying false positives
Escalating genuine threats to Tier 2

Tier 1 analysts are the first line of defense.

Tier 2 - Investigation and Incident Response

Tier 2 analysts handle alerts that have been escalated from Tier 1.

Their responsibilities include:

Performing deeper investigations
Analyzing evidence
Confirming security incidents
Coordinating incident response activities
Tier 3 - Advanced Analysis and Threat Hunting

Tier 3 analysts are experienced security experts.

They focus on:

Complex investigations
Advanced malware analysis
Threat hunting
Developing detection rules
Improving the organization's security posture
A Typical Tier 1 SOC Analyst Shift

Today's lesson described what a normal Tier 1 SOC Analyst does during a work shift.

A typical workflow includes:

1. Open the SIEM

Begin the shift by checking the SIEM dashboard for new security alerts.

2. Read Handover Notes

Review notes from the previous shift to understand ongoing investigations or unresolved incidents.

3. Check Security Alerts

Analyze newly generated alerts and determine whether they require investigation.

4. Investigate Suspicious Activity

Review logs, network activity, endpoint information, and other available evidence to understand what happened.

5. Close False Positives

Many alerts turn out to be harmless.

If an alert is determined to be a false positive, it is documented and closed.

6. Gather Evidence

If suspicious activity appears genuine, collect supporting evidence such as:

Logs
IP addresses
User activity
Network traffic
Timeline of events
7. Escalate Serious Incidents

If the investigation indicates a real threat, escalate the case to Tier 2 with clear documentation and supporting evidence.

Recognizing a Real Threat

One of the most important skills for a SOC analyst is distinguishing genuine attacks from routine or harmless activity.

Analysts must carefully evaluate alerts instead of assuming every alert represents a cyberattack.

This helps reduce unnecessary investigations while ensuring real threats are addressed quickly.

What is Triage?

Triage is the process of reviewing security alerts and deciding their priority.

During triage, analysts determine:

Is this alert real?
How serious is it?
Does it require immediate action?
Should it be escalated?

Good triage helps security teams focus on the highest-risk incidents first.

Why Documentation Matters

A SOC analyst's job is not complete after identifying a threat.

Every investigation should be documented clearly.

Good documentation includes:

What happened
Evidence collected
Investigation steps
Final conclusion
Actions taken

Accurate documentation allows other analysts to understand and continue the investigation if needed.

Why Communication Matters

Cybersecurity is a team effort.

SOC analysts must communicate their findings clearly so that:

Other analysts understand the situation.
Incident responders know what action to take.
Managers receive accurate information.
Future investigations benefit from previous work.

Clear communication is just as important as technical knowledge.
